My first few days in Tokyo were rather lack-luster. Between the jet lag and arriving sick, I was just not feeling great my first four or so days here.

I'm staying in Horinouchi, which is about 30-40 minutes from city center. It's quite quiet here. In retrospect I probably should have stayed in Shibuya or Shinjuku, closer to the action and other tourists. A lesson for future stays.

## Gyo-en National Garden
Once I started feeling better I got out to the [Gyo-en National Garden](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e3034_001.html) in Shinjuku. This garden dates back to the early Edo Period (1603-1868) and transferred hands a few times after that, but was well-damaged by those pesky Allied powers in WWII. It was rebuilt after the war sporting a tea garden, French-style garden, and greenhouse. Gnarly dude!

## Next stop, Meguro and Shibuya
I went to the TOP Museum -- which is a clumsy abbreviation of the TO(kyo) P(hotographic Art) Museum -- because I like photography, and because it was free. Boy was I duped though because upon arrival I learned that there is one free exhibit and two paid ones. Ya got me, TOP! I couldn't say no, though. Honestly, I was not terribly impressed, but boy was I excited when I walked out the back door to see this majesty.

{% include img.html src='/assets/images/tokyo-part-one-2026/big-red-ball-0001.jpg' %}
Apparently it's the office for a bus-driving school. Frankly, what the hell. Bus-driving schools should be shabbier than this gorgeous giant.

I'm so glad to have been hungry by then because I had the best ramen I've ever had that afternoon. Tomato and shrimp broth with pesto. Oh my god, so delicious. I've not had a better meal since this one. I was very confused coming in, though. A lot of these ramen joints have a ticketing system; You pay at an automated kiosk (sometimes digital, sometimes analog), which then prints out three pieces of paper, two of which seem to be receipts and one that's a ticket you show to the cook or watier, which just does not make sense to me. Frankly...what the hell.

In my search for other free things to do I found the Meguro Sky Garden. Lo and behold, it was actually free! No TOP-like predatory practices to be found here! Five stories up, it provided a decent view of the city. If it was clearer I'd be able to see Mt. Fuji, but alas...another day!
Pretty cool spot, though. The building wraps around a hollow center, and the garden path crawls slowly upwards toward the final city view. The architecture here is beautiful.

## Ikebukuro Fukuro Festival
Luckily, there is a website built for people who are bad at planning, like me, called [tokyocheapo.com](https://www.tokyocheapo.com) which alerted me to this dance festival in Ikebukuro. I got to see some traditional [Sado Okesa](https://www.visitsado.com/en/about-sado/tradition/) dancing and modern [Yosakoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosakoi) dancing. The Yosakoi dances were my favorite. They had huge umbrellas and flags as props, and the costumes were fun.

## What have I been up to?
I mean, this stuff. But have I been packing every day with adventure? Definitely not. I've spent entire days at my share house, only getting outside for a walk and a coffee. Even though I've done much more than I would have at home, I do feel like I'm not doing enough. However, looking at it differently, I'm grateful for the opportunity to take life slowly for a while.
